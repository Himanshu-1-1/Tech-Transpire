The Client

A healthcare compliance provider processing federally regulated patient survey data on behalf of hospital systems across the United States. A series of acquisitions had left the organization operating multiple sample processing engines, each running the same regulated program on a different platform.

The Challenge

Because more than one engine processed the same regulated program, the statistical sampling behind it varied depending on which platform a client happened to sit on. Clients were falling out of compliance through failures that originated with the provider rather than with them. One customer lost reimbursement as a result and came close to declaring bankruptcy.

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services had issued formal compliance warnings, carrying the prospect of fines or of shutting the business down. The consolidation onto a single engine was already seven years old and advancing at roughly ten client migrations per quarter, which put completion years further out.

What We Did

STEPS

Body

Handpicked and trained a 12-member team

Engineers, operations staff, and QA specialists were selected specifically for the knowledge the migration depended on, then trained on the process before the event rather than during it.

Piloted five client systems end to end

Five complete migrations were run in advance to validate the full path under production conditions and to find where time was actually being consumed. The event would execute a proven process rather than develop one.

Built tooling for rapid analysis and implementation

Purpose-built queries and procedures were developed to compress the analysis and implementation steps. Nineteen stored procedures were produced during the event itself as a by-product of the work.

Ran three days onsite with communication shut off

No email, no instant messaging, no phone calls. Nineteen hours of genuinely uninterrupted execution across three consecutive days, with participants physically in the same room.

Published hourly results company-wide

Progress dashboards were updated hourly and made visible to executives and to the wider organization outside the sessions. Visibility supplied both momentum inside the room and accountability outside it.

Left the method behind

The approach was documented and transferred rather than treated as a one-off intervention. Twelve other programs subsequently replicated it.

The Results

Processing engines for 47 hospital system clients, comprising 389 individual hospitals, were migrated within the 19-hour event. A seven-year-old initiative was closed two years ahead of its projected completion.

Retiring the legacy platform produced $311,000 in annual savings and improved quality by 50%. Data integrity was restored by consolidating onto a single system processing a single product, and the organization was returned to CMS compliance.

The commercial consequence mattered more than any of the operational metrics. With the compliance exposure resolved and the legacy platform gone, the division became a viable acquisition target and was subsequently acquired.
