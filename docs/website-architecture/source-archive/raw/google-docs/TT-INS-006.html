You Don't Have an Execution Problem. You're Executing the Wrong Plan.

This is the most important thing we tell new clients, and it is the hardest for them to hear: the problem is not that your team cannot execute. The problem is that you are executing something that was never correctly defined.

We have worked with mid-sized organizations that had strong teams, adequate budgets, experienced vendors, and reasonable timelines — and still ended up with failed implementations. Not because anyone was incompetent. Because the initiative was built on a flawed understanding of what the actual problem was.

The Cost of Executing the Wrong Plan

The cost of executing the wrong plan is always greater than the cost of slowing down to define the right one. Always. The rework, the delays, the loss of stakeholder confidence, the second implementation that has to undo the first — these are expensive in ways that do not show up in the original project budget.
